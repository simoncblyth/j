Specifications of "NVIDIA RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell server edition" 
-----------------------------------------------------------------

* full-height, full-length (FHFL), dual-slot, 10.5-inch PCIe Gen5 card
* up to 600 W maximum board power per card
* passively cooled, no fans : relies on rack cooling

  * MANDATORY : high-static-pressure cooling shrouds.

* with **PCIe Gen 5** support

My thoughts
~~~~~~~~~~~~

  * Gen 5 gives double the bandwidth of PCIe Gen 4
  * Each x16 slot provides up to 32 GB/s (Gen 4) or 64 GB/s (Gen 5) bidirectional 
  * upload/download to/from GPU will likely become the bottleneck for simulation
    so using a motherboard that supports PCIe Gen 5 can avoid a factor of 2 slowdown 



Chassis Question to Gemini
-----------------------------

Please list chassis models that are suitable for 8x and 4x deployments of RTX
Pro 6000 Blackwell series server edition GPUs.

My thoughts
~~~~~~~~~~~~

Power/thermal/cooling requirements for 8x "NVIDIA RTX 6000 Pro Blackwell server edition" 
are extreme requiring specific chassis that are certified to handle them. 
This is essential to avoid unreliability/throttling that would force using 
reduced power running.  

Splitting the 8x into 2 servers with 4x each would avoid the risks of provisioning 
for very high power/thermal/cooling requirements and allow more standard chassis to be used,
that you are probably familiar with already.

3. Critical Checklist for Your Purchase
The "Passive" Cooling Requirement

The Server Edition of the RTX 6000 Blackwell has no internal fans.

    Warning: You cannot put these in a standard workstation or a generic rack server without high-static-pressure cooling shrouds.

    Verify: Ensure the chassis specifically lists support for 600W passive GPUs.

The PCIe Root Complex

For Opticks, you want the GPUs to be as "close" to the CPU as possible.

    In 8-GPU systems, ask if the chassis uses a Single-Root (all 8 GPUs on one CPU) or Dual-Root (4 GPUs per CPU) design.

    Geant4 Tip: A Dual-Root design is usually better for Geant4 because it allows more CPU memory bandwidth to feed the cards simultaneously.

Power Infrastructure

Each 8-GPU server will draw nearly 5,500W - 6,000W at full load (including CPUs and fans).

    You will likely need L6-30P or IEC C19/C20 power cables.

    Standard 110V/15A wall outlets will not support these servers; you will need 208V-240V high-amperage PDUs.







Motherboard Question to Gemini
--------------------------------

Please list motherboards that are suitable for 8x and 4x deployments of RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell series server edition GPUs.
Which specifications are particularly import for Geant4/Opticks simulation workloads ?


My Thoughts
~~~~~~~~~~~

* support for PCIe Gen 5.0 x16 slots is crucial (to avoid a 2x slowdown in
  transfers between CPU and GPU which will likely become the bottleneck for simulation)

* complexity and cost of dual processor boards with PCIe Gen 5.0 switches 
  is needed to avoid communicating with 8 cards becoming a continuous traffic 
  jam on the bus limiting the potential of the server. Again splitting
  the 8x cards across two servers would avoid this complexity and bottleneck risk.


Gemini
~~~~~~~

For Geant4/Opticks simulations, the motherboard is more than just a mounting
point; it acts as the primary data highway. Since Opticks offloads the most
computationally expensive part (optical photon propagation) while Geant4
manages the world state on the CPU, the "distance" and bandwidth between the
two are your primary performance levers.


## **1. Suitable Motherboards for 4x and 8x Blackwell Deployments**

For the **RTX 6000 Blackwell Server Edition**, you generally use enterprise
server boards rather than consumer boards, as these cards are passive and
require specific airflow and power delivery found in rackmount-specific
motherboards.

### **For 4x GPU Deployments (Workstation/Tower or 2U)**

These are ideal for "small-scale" clusters or high-performance individual nodes
where you want to maximize CPU-to-GPU bandwidth.

* **ASUS Pro WS WRX90E-SAGE SE:** This is the current "gold standard" for 4-GPU workstation builds. 
  It supports **AMD Threadripper PRO 7000/9000** series and provides **7x PCIe Gen 5.0 x16 slots**. 
  You can run 4x Blackwell cards at full x16 Gen 5 speeds with ease.

* **GIGABYTE MH53-G40:** 
  An enterprise-grade board for AMD Threadripper PRO, often used in the Gigabyte W773-H5D chassis. 
  It is built for sustained 24/7 simulation loads.

* **GIGABYTE MW83-RP0:** 
  The Intel equivalent for **Xeon W-2400/W-3400** processors, suitable for 4x GPU deployments.


### **For 8x GPU Deployments (4U/Enterprise)**

Motherboards for 8-GPU setups are typically integrated into "barebone" 
server systems because they require complex PCIe switching.

* **ASUS ESC8000A-E13X Board:** 
  A dual-socket **AMD EPYC 9005 (Turin)** board. 
  It is one of the few that features **PCIe Gen 6 switches** 
  to manage the massive traffic between 8x Blackwell GPUs without bottlenecking the CPU.

* **Supermicro H13DGU:** 
  A dual-processor board found in the SYS-522GA-NRT. 
  It is designed to handle the 600W power draw and high heat density of 8 passive GPUs.

* **GIGABYTE XL44-SX2 Board:** 
  Part of the **NVIDIA MGX** modular architecture, designed specifically to pair with Blackwell 
  and BlueField-3 DPUs for high-speed simulation networking.

---

## **2. Vital Specs for Geant4/Opticks Workloads**

When selecting a board for Geant4/Opticks, prioritize these technical specs to
avoid "starving" your GPUs:

### **PCIe Lane Topology (The "Bottleneck" Spec)**

Opticks is highly sensitive to **PCIe latency**.

* **Requirement:** Ensure the board provides **x16 Gen 5** lanes to every slot.

* **Pro Tip:** Look for "Dual-Root" topology. In an 8-GPU setup, a Dual-Root
  board connects 4 GPUs to CPU #1 and 4 GPUs to CPU #2. This doubles the
  effective bandwidth for moving Geant4 event data from the system RAM to the
  GPUs compared to a Single-Root system.

### **Memory Channels & Bandwidth**

Geant4 is a memory-intensive CPU task. If the CPU can't process the initial
particle steps fast enough, the GPUs will sit idle waiting for the next batch
of optical photons.

* **Requirement:** Use boards with **12-channel memory** (available on AMD EPYC 9004/9005 and Intel Xeon 6).
* **Capacity:** Opticks batch sizes can be huge. Aim for at least **2x to 4x
  the total VRAM** in system RAM (e.g., for 4x 96GB GPUs, aim for 1TB of System RAM).

### **RDMA & DPU Support (BlueField-3)**

If you are running these across a cluster via Slurm, the "hand-off" between nodes matters.

* **Importance:** Boards that support **BlueField-3 DPUs** or **ConnectX-7/8**
  allow for **GPUDirect RDMA**. This lets one server's GPU talk to another's
  without involving the CPU, which is a massive speedup for large-scale
  distributed detector simulations.

### **Power Delivery (VRM Quality)**

The RTX 6000 Blackwell can spike to **600W**.

* **Requirement:** The motherboard must have high-quality VRMs and enough
  **EPS/PCIe 8-pin or 16-pin power headers** to stabilize the board's own power
  draw when all 8 PCIe slots are fully saturated.
